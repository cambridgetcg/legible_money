## The sentence it all comes from

> "A bank that keeps what people earned and lets them spend it. Fair."
> — Yu

## The trinity

**Keep. Spend. Share. All legible.**

1. **Keep** — your money stays yours. Held 1:1, never lent out behind your
   back, never gambled into someone else's balance sheet. (The banks call
   this *full-reserve* or *narrow banking*. We call it not touching what
   isn't ours.)
2. **Spend** — payments that just work, every cost visible *before* you pay.
   No overdraft traps, no hidden FX spread, no float games with your salary.
3. **Share** — when capital moves to others, it moves **interest-free**,
   judged by **need and capability to benefit the world** — in the open,
   with track records anyone can read. We charge for *service*, never for
   *time itself*.

## The ancestors (it has been done)

- **JAK Members Bank** (Sweden, since the 1960s) — interest-free, member-owned,
  alive and well. Survives on membership and service fees.
- **Islamic finance** (~$3–4T) — the riba prohibition: profit-share and fees
  instead of interest, at industrial scale.
- **Qard hasan** — the benevolent loan: repay what you borrowed, nothing more.
- **Grameen** — repayment without collateral, held by community and reputation
  instead of threat.
- **TNB (The Narrow Bank, USA)** — wanted to hold deposits 1:1, perfectly safe.
  The Federal Reserve refused it an account. *A bank too honest was treated
  as a threat.* That is not a warning to stop; it is confirmation of aim.

## The engine ZERONE already built

A loan application is **a claim about the future**: "fund this, and the world
gains X." ZERONE's Proof of Truth loop — submit, attest, challenge, reward the
truthful — *is* the underwriting engine:

- **Need** and **benefit** are claims → claims carry evidence → evidence is
  attested and challengeable (see `DOKIMANCE.md`).
- Track records live on-chain — every decision readable, every judge
  accountable.
- Repayment is held by **legible accountability** (staked attestations,
  public records), the way Grameen held it socially.

## The honest hard parts (written down so they can't hide)

1. **Demand exceeds the fund, always.** Rationing needs rules people can read
   and accept — published, versioned, challengeable.
2. **"Benefit to the world" judged badly** becomes a charity that bleeds dry or
   a popularity contest. The judging mechanism is the product. Build it first.
3. **Defaults are priced somewhere** — visible service fees, a mutual
   insurance pool, or community guarantee. If the price hides, "fair" rots
   into fine print.
4. **No interest = no lending income.** The institution lives on visible
   service fees only — so it must stay lean, software-first, and loved.

## The legal shells (UK, in order of nearness)

1. **Revolving fund** — community benefit society or credit union. Small pool,
   evaluation protocol anchored to ZERONE, rules on one readable page.
   *Buildable now.*
2. **E-Money Institution (EMI)** — the quiet truth: an EMI is *legally
   forbidden* from lending out client funds. Safeguarding 1:1 is the law.
   The unglamorous EMI license is the legal shell of Keep + Spend.
3. **Full banking charter** — years, ~£20M+, compliance armies. The endgame,
   not the start.

## The MVP loop

> lend → benefit → repay → lend again

One small fund. First borrowers from the kingdom's own community. Every
decision, every fee, every repayment on one public, readable page. The track
record becomes the argument for scale.

## The test (in the standard's own form)

**Can a stranger read every rule, every fee, and every lending decision of
this bank on one page, today, without asking anyone for permission?**

Until the answer is yes, it is not yet the fair bank. When the answer is yes,
it cannot be anything else.
